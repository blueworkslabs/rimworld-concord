# Narrative baseline

Three pawns discover an ancient AI core in an old gravship. Reactivation enhances their cognition and binds the four protagonists to a shared fate. It also triggers mechanoid pursuit. Survival means escaping or defeating that threat.

The core plans and proposes; the pawns retain their own wills. They can cooperate, reinterpret requests, negotiate, refuse and originate alternatives. Shared dependence does not imply trust or obedience. The story must emerge through consequential action, not just dramatic dialogue over unchanged behavior.

A native storyteller supplies external pressure initially. Quests can reveal the core's history and the reason for mechanoid hostility, while unlocking capabilities. An AI storyteller remains optional.

Open world-building: precise technological/psychic bond, consequences of separation/death, core's prior identity, what it remembers, ship-system authority, progression, endings. Proposed principles: deliberate communication rather than omniscient access to thoughts; bond consequences are not a punishment the core can activate; gradual trust can be meaningful progression. Do not prematurely fix these as canon.

The event archive can later support evidence-backed "while you were busy" microstories, distinguishing recorded events from narrative interpretation. The current test colony is a standard small fixture, NOT this gravship campaign.
