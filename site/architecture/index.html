How Concord works

Language models are good at choosing and explaining. They're bad at being trusted with authority. So Concord gives them neither the game nor the database. Each mind gets a narrow view, a menu of real options, and a structured reply. Everything that matters is decided and checked underneath.

The system map

Two paths into one game. The character path runs through the coordinator, where consent and memory live. The player harness is a separate, narrower path that a benchmark controller uses today and the core is meant to take over in phase 2. On both, authority flows downward only as validated actions, and truth flows upward as observations and receipts.

01 · Consent loop

Offers, answers, fresh consent

The core never touches a colonist. It creates a proposal bound to one pawn, and only that pawn's decision handle can answer it: accept, refuse, counter or not now. Anything else, like a forged actor or an extra field, is rejected before it reaches the game. This is the character path; the player harness below has no consent step yet, which is what phase 2 C restores.

A counter executes nothing. If the core adopts it, it comes back as a revised offer that needs a fresh yes, with at most two revisions per thread. Replacing a stockpile haul with rescue needs fresh consent, confirmed exclusion from the haul, and an empty-handed pawn off the captured haul trip; already-carried cargo finishes natively before rescue can start. An answer that arrives after the work is already done lapses: it is kept as given, but starts nothing.

An accepted haul becomes a native intent; the ordered haul was retired. Rescue, building and cooking still use the older ordered model, one hand-built job that stops on low needs.

02 · Knowledge boundaries

Who knows what

Each mind gets a scoped perspective; the core uses an explicit allowlist rather than receiving pawn or character state wholesale. A colonist sees its own traits, skills, exact needs, surviving memories, its own history and a private outlook of up to four revisable notes, each tied to its own experiences.

The core receives explicitly shared information: addressed messages, agreement receipts, grounded opportunities, attributed local food/campfire sightings, and shared-link Food/Rest telemetry (urgent, low, satisfied, unknown) with a timestamp. Telemetry is not a visual observation. Stale readings become unknown, never zero, and the core view distinguishes observation ticks from event/receipt times.

What the colony has built (stockpiles, blueprints, bills) is colony-public, because someone placed it on the map. Loose things stay attributed sightings: a native work scan seeing the whole map is not permission to put unobserved resources in a model's menu.

03 · Native intents

Steer the planner, don't drive the pawn

RimWorld already has an intent layer: the player's controls. Stockpile zones, blueprints, bills and designations are state on the map, and pawns fulfil them through their own work givers, in between eating and sleeping. Concord now uses that layer. An accepted haul becomes a stockpile tagged with its agreement and a quota ("up to 30 wood"), not a stream of orders.

Harmony hooks report job starts and ends, eating and actual placements. Credit comes from placements, per pawn: credited plus reserved never exceeds the quota, and anything else that lands in the zone is counted as incidental, never hidden. The tag sits on the zone, not the wood. Trips already under way before tagging remain ordinary work and get no agreement credit; withdrawal blocks new pickups but lets already-carried cargo finish. A pawn who refused or deferred is excluded from new tagged work; anyone else who pitches in is labelled helping, not asked. When the quota is met, one record retires the agreement and the zone becomes an ordinary stockpile.

The core never sets work priorities, and a pawn who can't do a kind of work is visibly not offered it. The loads are strict: one source per trip, no extra pickups on the way; a growing hold is parked after it failed to come through two fix rounds cleanly.

04 · Player harness

Play like a player, minus the mouse

The core kept failing to turn available opportunities into action. Its narrow view is a strong, testable explanation, though not a proven sole cause: no campfire, no beds, a medical alert and 120 wood by the wall were all things a player could see and it couldn't. So phase 1 asked a plainer question: can an agent run the colony better through a structured interface than through the human UI?

The harness reads what a player can read: one coherent snapshot per read with stable IDs, a byte-bounded digest with a change marker, and queries such as a placement check that uses the game's own build designator. Hidden weather schedules, fogged cells and storyteller plans stay out. It acts through what a player can use: blueprints, designations, zones, bills, work priorities, schedules, forbid and allow, area restrictions, bed assignment and time controls. No drafting and no direct pawn jobs; the game's own work givers do the work.

Every action returns an acceptance receipt or refusal reason, identifying whether it came from the game or harness validation. An accepted blueprint is not a built campfire, and assigning a bed is not sleeping: completion is read later from game state. Since phase 2 A, each accepted edit also writes one narrated record naming the core as decision-maker, never an invented pawn agreement. That narration is scripted-tested with zero model calls; the sealed viewer-legibility test is still ahead.

05 · Model minds

Small views, real menus, no tools

A decision is one bounded call. The model gets its perspective, where every need says what its scale means, plus a menu of executable choices with their effects spelled out. Contextual schemas constrain choices and supplied IDs where supported; runtime validation still checks every reply. Counterproposal actions have additional execution-time checks. Shared instructions are supplemented by relevant action and knowledge contracts.

The Claude route runs with no tools, no MCP servers, no plugins, and checks that at start-up before every call. Every trial has a durable attempt ledger and no rerolls. Failures keep their reserved attempt and a bounded diagnostic stage. Backends are swappable: scripted, Claude, Luna (now the core and pawn model in continuous play) and Jev for fast appraisal. A failed request records its cause, including prompts that are too large before any model is called.

06 · Attention and time

Three speeds of thought

In continuous mode, native habits such as eating, sleeping and ordinary jobs can continue while a colonist deliberates, with a thinking badge over their head. Explicit inference pauses stop simulation. Fast appraisal decides whether an event deserves a thought (bounded live trials so far; not in recent runs). Deliberation handles negotiation, reconsideration and planning.

Native events are archived and routed only to the pawn who experienced them. Repeated signals coalesce, health events bypass cooldowns, and mundane chatter waits instead of cancelling a thought in progress. Reflection pacing spreads thinking over the observation window. Failures are counted per lane (core, decisions, answers, reflections), so a success in one lane can't hide repeated failures in another, and oversized reflection views are trimmed oldest-first rather than raising the limit. Play is continuous by default; pausing at decisions is an explicit test mode.

07 · The core

A planner that waits to be needed

Each core turn picks one of four moves: propose a listed opportunity, adopt a counter for fresh consent, ask one pawn one question, or wait. It keeps up to eight sourced topics marked open, blocked, deferred, resolved or declined. "Resolved" requires completed linked work, so its prose can't close a topic that the receipts don't close.

It wakes on public changes: finished or stopped work, requests, messages, answers, and native-intent milestones or stalls. Opportunity churn alone does not wake it. A shared status band changing on its own, with no offerable opportunity or counter, is handled silently, with no model call and no crew-log entry, just a "waiting on…" status line, unless Food or Rest reaches urgent (including a first or previously unknown reading). Private needs do not wake it. After an explicit wait while work remains offerable, at most two bounded reviews are allowed (2,500 ticks, then 5,000); otherwise idle time stays silent. A wait never writes to the log. Messages published after newer receipts carry an "as of" mark. Refused work isn't pushed again; a colonist who said "not now" can invite exactly one fresh offer.

08 · Timeline safety

Saves, reloads and slow thoughts

Model calls take seconds; players save, load and rewind. Every loaded game gets a fresh epoch, so an answer from before a reload can't act in the new timeline. Each accepted action gets an ID before dispatch, and the game's saved ledger makes a duplicate a no-op.

Paired checkpoints save the paused game, hash it and store the matching character state together. Restoring checks the hash and forks the timeline. Gameplay reports distinguish paired restore from a cold coordinator restore: a new coordinator process (not necessarily a game restart), with retained results and no extra model calls. Some experiments left restore gaps; offline checks do not demonstrate native persistence.

09 · Evidence culture

Every claim has a receipt

  • Scripted game tests for every mechanic, including refusal, withdrawal and restore.
  • Frozen live trials, setup signed up front, no rerolls, failures kept, recorded uncut and read cold before any technical findings are released.
  • Three gates per migration: an internals note from the game's own assemblies, a signed design before code, and a verdict after runs.
  • Offline snapshot banks: the same situation, varied one thing at a time, across models.
  • Offline typed-claims prototype that distinguishes submitted facts, forecasts and preferences; it is not a production truth checker.
  • Uncut recordings: the first native haul, the hauling migration's first run and Gate C rerun, a pipeline regression, ten minutes of choosing to eat and a scripted harness-actions check.
  • Independent code review on every nontrivial PR and fact-checked diary entries for selected milestones.

Well-formed output and grounded understanding are measured separately. Passing the schema doesn't count as understanding.

Known gaps, stated plainly

A successful benchmark is not yet a watchable crew. Eighteen scored arms completed three frozen tasks. The isolated player controller can build and cook, but the in-world core has not yet been integrated with that harness. No new integrated multi-step scene or binding-refusal trial is claimed.

The old native-haul verdict is complete, not pending. The game-side migration passed, while missing character conflict and narrative weaknesses remain explicit. The ordered hauling runtime was retired; the separate construction-consent slice is retired unmerged, with its partial evidence retained. The agreed phase-2 plan now governs what follows.

What comes next is measured separately. Receipt-backed action narration has scripted/UI evidence, not a sealed four-of-five viewer verdict. T4 still needs completed-tending evidence and simultaneous actual sleep under roofs. Public-core knowledge limits and a versioned, opt-in annotation join precede the core integration; pawn-private views remain separate.

Hauling runs strict. The growing hold, which would keep RimWorld's extra pickups within the quota, is parked after its two fix rounds; the in-game clock is verified on one map only.

Character continuity is early. Outlooks form, persist and show up in later reasoning. Whether they add up to stable personalities over a long game is an open question.

It's a lab build, not a player download. The mod only activates in a configured lab environment.
