Three colonists find an ancient AI core in an old gravship. Waking it sharpens their minds and binds all four of them together. The core can watch, reason and propose. It cannot command. Every plan needs a colonist's yes, and a no is a real answer.

The idea

What if the colonists had to agree?

RimWorld already tells great stories. Its colonists have traits, needs, grudges and breakdowns. But they don't make plans. They follow work priorities you set, and the drama comes from what happens to them.

Concord adds a layer of deliberate choice on top. An AI core offers work, asks questions and follows up on unfinished business. Each colonist weighs that offer against their own needs, memories and outlook, and can take it, bargain it down, turn it down, or say "not now". Whatever they agree to becomes an ordinary RimWorld job, so walking, eating, hauling and bleeding stay exactly as the game does them.

The upgrade isn't smarter pathfinding. It shows up in what they choose, and in being able to read why.

The protagonists

One core, three colonists

Four protagonists, none in charge. The test colony below is a small fixture in the desert, not the gravship campaign yet.

The Core

Proposes, asks, remembers

Offers grounded work, adopts counteroffers, asks one question at a time and keeps track of open topics. It sees what an attentive crewmate could see, never thoughts, and it wakes only when something in the colony changes.

Alvin

The negotiator, so far

Cautious and self-monitoring. He talks a haul down when his food is low and ties his choices to what his crewmates told him.

Beatrice

Brisk and accommodating

Takes full jobs without haggling and offered to cook if someone got a fire going. She's been downed, rescued and hungry, and she says so.

Pedro

The quiet third

Mostly a bystander in early trials. The campfire milestone gives him the same choices as everyone else, including saying no.

How it works

Three rules keep it honest

They aren't in the prompts; they're enforced in code, beneath the models. A model can pick an option or say something. It can't grant itself permission.

The core proposes, colonists decide

Every offer gets an answer: accept, counter, refuse or not now. A counter comes back as a fresh offer that needs a fresh yes. Nobody can agree on someone else's behalf.

The game is the ground truth

Accepted work becomes a real native job with a receipt. What a colonist says is testimony; what happened comes from the game. "My haul is wrapped up" doesn't count as done unless the receipts agree.

Minds stay private

Traits, memories and a small, revisable private outlook shape each choice. The crew log shows speech and records, never thoughts. The core gets coarse bands like "food: low", not the meter.

The target

What ten minutes of watching should feel like

This is the experience we're building toward, not a description of today's build. If a viewer can retell it in four sentences and say who disagreed and why, it works.

  1. 0–2 minA need surfaces in the open. Someone says it, or a shared status band turns amber.
  2. 2–4 minThe core proposes a plan in pieces. Build a campfire, then cook. At least one colonist bargains or refuses, and it costs something.
  3. 4–7 minMostly quiet execution. The log says what everyone is waiting on, so silence reads as patience, not a stall.
  4. 7–9 minAn interruption forces a rethink. Hunger, a downed crewmate, something on the horizon.
  5. 9–10 minA payoff you can see. A lit fire, a meal on the ground, and one thread left open for next time.
Where we are

An honest status report

Experimental vertical slice, not a playable mod yet

Consent, negotiation, hauling, rescue, campfire construction and cooking, a readable crew log, private outlooks, pawn-to-pawn messages and a first live, event-driven core all work in bounded trials, some live and some scripted. Every run has a fixed call budget and no rerolls, and failures stay on the record.

Not there yet: the live core turning a need into a multi-step plan (the campfire chain works in scripted tests but hasn't happened live), unpaused real-time play, long sessions, the gravship campaign and an installer for players.

Later

Where it's heading

The gravship campaign

Waking the core also wakes mechanoid pursuit. Quests reveal what the core was, and trust can become progression. Still a proposal, not canon.

You, inside the fiction

Send the core a suggestion, write to a colonist, or catch one at the comms console for a live chat. You get advice and persuasion, never remote control.

Cheaper minds

The interface is being hardened so smaller, cheaper models can run the colonists. The first cross-model checks are already in the repo.
